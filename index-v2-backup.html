What I found

01

Your homepage loads about 60 MB, and your veneers page is slow on a simulated phone.

On October 23, 2026, Lighthouse (a Google tool, simulating a Moto G Power phone on a slow connection) loaded your homepage at 60,305 KiB, about 59 MB. The main content, the photo of Dr. Silva Battaglin and Dr. Adam Persky, finished at 21.6 seconds and the performance score was 65 out of 100. Your veneers page loaded 21,566 KiB, finished at 42.3 seconds and scored 45. The biggest files are photos: a 4.7 MB image on the homepage, and on the veneers page the two before and after pictures (2.6 MB and 2.5 MB each) and several more images of 2.3 to 2.4 MB each. Lighthouse estimates that newer image formats would save about 29,090 KiB on the homepage and that sizing images to the space they fill would save about 43,709 KiB. A real phone on a fast connection will do better than this test, and I can't tell what your visitors actually get.

02

Every address on your site returns the same page file, even ones that don't exist.

I fetched all 16 pages in your sitemap without running scripts. Every one returned the same 10,109-byte file with the same title, “Cosmetic Dentist Las Vegas | Smile Sketch Vegas”, the same canonical address (your homepage) and no readable text, because the words load afterwards by script. In a browser each page does show its own title and headline. An address that doesn't exist, /this-page-does-not-exist-xyz123, also answers 200 and draws a page titled “This Page Does Not Exist Xyz123 | Smile Sketch Vegas — Dr. Adam Persky, DMD”. The addresses /llms.txt and /smilesketchvegashome1 return the same file with a 200. In a browser, your homepage and your Results page both name /home as their canonical address, and the Results page has no headline. I can't tell how Google treats these pages, or whether ChatGPT, Perplexity or other AI tools run the scripts that fill them in.
